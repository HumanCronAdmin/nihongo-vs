〜ねえ (nee) vs 〜ない (nai)

What's the Difference?

Quick Answer

Use 〜ねえ for casual, spoken negation, and 〜ない for standard, written negation. 〜ねえ is often used in informal conversations, while 〜ない is more versatile. Choose based on context and formality.

Side-by-Side Comparison

Aspect 〜ねえ (nee) 〜ない (nai)

Formality Informal, casual Neutral, suitable for most situations

〜ねえ (nee)

JLPT: N5 | Formality: casual

  • Formality: Informal, casual
  • Pronunciation: Slurred, contracted form of 〜ない
  • Usage: Mainly used in spoken language, especially in informal conversations

〜ない (nai)

JLPT: N5 | Formality: neutral

  • Formality: Neutral, suitable for most situations
  • Pronunciation: Standard, full pronunciation
  • Usage: Used in both spoken and written language, including formal writing

Example Sentences

〜ねえ (nee)

食べねえ

tabenee

I won't eat

行くねえ

ikune

I'm not going

知らねえ

shiranee

I don't know

〜ない (nai)

食べない

tabenai

I don't eat

行かない

ikanai

I won't go

知らない

shiranai

I don't know

Common Mistakes

  • ⚠ Using 〜ねえ in formal writing or professional settings, which can come across as too casual or unprofessional.
  • ⚠ Confusing the pronunciation of 〜ねえ with 〜ない, leading to incorrect usage.
  • ⚠ Overusing 〜ねえ in conversations, which can make the speaker sound overly casual or lazy.

Memory Tip

💡 Remember that 〜ねえ sounds like 'neh' and is used like 'not gonna' in casual conversations, while 〜ない is the standard 'not' used in most situations.
